## 1. Policy statement and scope

Chorlton Repair Cafe will keep everyone at our sessions safe: no repair is worth an injury. We are a volunteer-run community group, and we will take all reasonably practicable steps to prevent harm to volunteers, visitors and venue users. A typical session has around 20 volunteers and 50 visitors.

This policy applies to every repair cafe session, currently 10:00–12:00 on the second Saturday of each month at St Margaret's Community Centre, Brantingham Road, Chorlton, M21 0TT. It also covers set-up, pack-down and any pop-up or outreach events we run elsewhere. At pop-up events away from St Margaret's, soldering irons and hot air guns may be used, with the same hot work precautions as at our venue.

It applies to:

- **Organisers** – the core team who run the cafe
- **Volunteers** – repairers, welcome desk, refreshments and helpers
- **Visitors** – people bringing items, and anyone accompanying them, including children

We will:

- Carry out and review a written risk assessment for our sessions
- Give every volunteer a safety briefing and this policy before they repair
- Provide suitable tools, first aid and fire safety arrangements
- Record and learn from every accident, incident and near miss
- Never pressure a volunteer to attempt a repair they think is unsafe or beyond their skill

We are an unincorporated voluntary community group run on a not-for-profit basis. We do not make or sell anything; we repair people's own items and hand them back.

## 3. Risk assessment and venue set-up

We keep a written risk assessment for our sessions, reviewed every year and after any significant incident or change of venue. Our insurer requires a formal risk assessment and a training plan for volunteers. The Session Lead completes this checklist before doors open at 10:00.

**Pre-session checklist**

- Fire exits and routes are clear and unlocked; volunteers told where the assembly point is
- First aid kit present and stocked; First Aider identified
- Tables are stable; electrical repair tables sit near sockets so no cables cross walkways
- Extension leads are fully unwound, not daisy-chained, and fitted with RCD protection
- Soldering stations have a heat-resistant mat and stand, away from table edges and children
- Ventilation is adequate (windows open or extraction on) where soldering or glues are used
- Refreshments area is separate from repair tables; hot drinks are not carried across repair areas
- Sharps bin / box is available for blades and broken glass
- Signs are up: "Repairs at your own risk", "Supervise children", and fire exits
- Volunteer safety briefing given (5 minutes)

**Layout principles**

- Keep walkways at least 1 metre wide and free of bags, items and cables
- Give heavy or bulky repairs (bikes, vacuum cleaners) floor space away from the entrance
- Lift heavy items with two people; do not lift anything you cannot manage comfortably. Do not use ladders or work above 2 metres: our insurance does not cover work at height
- Keep the welcome desk at the entrance so items are screened before they reach a repair table

## 4. Safe repair practice

Repairers only take on repairs they are competent and confident to do; "I don't think it's safe" is always an acceptable answer.

**Electrical items**

- Never work on an item while it is plugged in. Unplug it and keep the plug in sight.
- Visually inspect every mains item before and after repair: plug, cable, cable grip, casing, fuse rating.
- Every electrical item leaving the cafe after a repair is PAT tested before it is returned, and the result is recorded on the repair sign-out form. Only volunteers qualified in PAT testing, or who have completed at least a one-day course in In-service Inspection and Testing of Electrical Equipment, may carry out the test. This is a condition of our insurance. Testing is done by our qualified PAT testers: Mark Hobbs, Harvinder Atwal and Jayne Cartwright.
- Only power a repaired item for testing through an RCD-protected socket or lead.
- Do not open microwave ovens (high-voltage capacitor), CRT TVs or monitors, or gas appliances.
- Discharge capacitors before working inside power supplies and appliances, if trained to do so.
- If an item fails the safety check, it is returned unrepaired with a "Not safe to use" label, and the owner is told why.

**Batteries**

- Treat lithium-ion batteries as a fire risk. Do not puncture, crush, short or charge a swollen or damaged battery.
- Place any swollen, hot or smoking battery in the [sand bucket / fire-safe container] and move it outdoors if safe to do so.

**Tools, heat and chemicals**

- Use the right tool for the job; keep blades sharp, sheathed and put away when not in use.
- Soldering irons go back in their stand every time; switch off when you leave the table.
- Wear safety glasses when cutting, prying, drilling, using springs or working with glass.
- Use glues, solvents and lubricants in ventilated areas, follow the label, and keep them away from children and food.
- Hot glue guns are treated like soldering irons. Hot air guns are only used with our insurer's hot work precautions in place: combustible material cleared from around the work, two fire extinguishers at hand, and a named person watching for smoke or fire during use and for 60 minutes afterwards.

**Textiles, bikes and other repairs**

- Sewing machine users keep fingers clear of the needle and unplug the machine before threading or changing needles.
- Pins and needles go back in a pincushion or tin, never on the table or in mouths.
- Bike repairs use a stand where possible; wear gloves for chains and greasy parts. We do not repair e-bikes or e-scooters: they are not covered by our insurance.
- No repairs to brakes on cars or motorbikes, or to safety-critical equipment such as climbing gear, car seats or helmets.

**Items we will not accept**

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Gas appliances and anything with fuel (petrol mowers, camping stoves with canisters) | Fire and explosion risk; gas work needs Gas Safe registration |
| Microwave ovens | Lethal high-voltage capacitor |
| CRT TVs and monitors | Lethal stored charge |
| E-bikes and e-scooters (any part), and swollen or damaged lithium batteries | Not covered by our insurance; fire risk |
| Items that are dirty with food, mould, bodily fluids or pest infestation | Hygiene |
| Medical devices and safety-critical items (helmets, car seats, smoke alarms) | Liability and life safety |
| Firearms, weapons, or anything the Session Lead judges unsafe | Safety |

The Session Lead has the final say on whether an item is accepted.

**Personal protective equipment (PPE)**

We provide safety glasses, disposable gloves and [heat-resistant gloves / dust masks]. Volunteers must use PPE where the task needs it, and tell the Session Lead if any is missing.

## 5. First aid, incidents and emergencies

If someone is seriously hurt, call 999 first, then the First Aider. Every injury, however small, and every near miss is recorded the same day.

**First aid**

- At least one volunteer with current first aid training is present at every session: [name(s), certificate expiry].
- The first aid kit is kept at [location] and checked by the H&S Leads every [3 months].
- Contents include plasters, sterile dressings, burn gel or dressings, eyewash, gloves and a foil blanket.
- The nearest defibrillator (AED) is at [location – to confirm].
- For burns: cool under cool running water for 20 minutes. For electric shock: do not touch the person until the power is off.

**Reporting accidents, incidents and near misses**

1. Make the area safe and give first aid.
2. Tell the Session Lead.
3. Record it in the accident book / incident form on the day: date, time, who, what happened, injury, action taken, witnesses.
4. The H&S Leads review every entry within 7 days and update the risk assessment if needed.
5. The H&S Leads notify our insurance broker or the insurer's claims team as soon as possible about anything that could lead to a claim, and send full details within 30 days.
6. Tell the venue about any incident involving their building or equipment.

Never admit fault or promise compensation at the time of an incident; pass it to the H&S Leads. Any letter of claim must be acknowledged within 21 days and a copy of the letter and our reply sent to the insurer. Any court papers go straight to the insurer, unanswered.

Records are stored securely by the H&S Leads and kept for at least [3 years], in line with insurance and data protection requirements.

**Fire and evacuation**

- On hearing the fire alarm, everyone stops work, switches off soldering irons if it is immediate and safe, and leaves by the nearest exit.
- Do not stop to collect items or belongings.
- The Session Lead takes the sign-in sheet to the assembly point at [location] and checks everyone is out.
- Do not re-enter until the fire service or venue says it is safe.
- Only tackle a small fire with an extinguisher if you are trained, it is safe, and your exit is behind you. For a battery fire, evacuate.

## 6. Safeguarding, insurance and training

**Children and vulnerable adults**

- We do not run activities for under-16s. Children who come with a parent or carer are supervised by them at all times, do not take part in repairs, and are kept away from soldering irons, hot air guns and tools.
- Volunteers are never left alone with a child or vulnerable adult. Our liability insurance excludes claims arising from abuse, so these rules protect both visitors and the cafe.
- Any safeguarding concern goes to the Safeguarding Lead: [name].

**Visitor disclaimer**

Every visitor signs in and agrees to our house rules before their item is looked at. The disclaimer explains that repairs are free, carried out by volunteers on a best-efforts basis, at the owner's own risk, and that we are not liable for loss or damage to items brought in, except where caused by our negligence. Our insurance does not cover a repaired item that fails to work or a repair that turns out to be faulty, and may not cover damage to visitors' items while in our care, so every visitor must sign it.

**Insurance**

We hold employers' liability insurance (which covers our volunteers) of £10 million, and public and products liability insurance of £5 million. A copy of the employers' liability certificate is displayed at every session, and copies of the policy are held by the H&S Leads.

The policy has conditions we must follow, or cover can be refused:

- **This policy** must be up to date and acknowledged by every volunteer.
- **PAT testing** of repaired electrical items by qualified or trained testers (section 4).
- **Hot work** precautions for hot air guns, at our venue and at pop-ups (section 4).
- **No work at height** above 2 metres; no ladders.
- **Prompt notification** of incidents and claims (section 5).

Refreshments (tea, coffee and cake) are covered. E-bikes are not covered and are not repaired. The insurance does not pay for a repair that fails to work or the cost of putting it right.

**Training and induction**

Before their first session as a repairer, each new volunteer:

- Reads this policy and confirms they have read it (below)
- Is shown the fire exits, first aid kit and incident book
- Repairs alongside an experienced volunteer for at least one session
- Is assessed for electrical testing before carrying out safety checks alone

**Review**

The H&S Leads review this policy every 12 months, and after any serious incident, change of venue or change of insurance. The review takes place each November, before the insurance renews on 11 December. Next review due: November 2027.
